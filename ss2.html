BMK’s Sahaja Siddha

A journey from family food needs to living biodiversity

About BMK’s Sahaja Siddha

BMK’s Sahaja Siddha began not as a project or business but as a natural farming effort without a name under the guidance of Beegala Malakondaiah, with one goal:

  • To provide pure and safe food for the family

In the early phase, cultivation was on nearly 1 acre of land, growing rice, vegetables, fruits, and trees—all without chemicals. Traditional farming principles were followed, respecting the soil as a living system.

A Turning Point – 2018

In 2018, a conversation with neighbouring farmer Penchalaiah brought to light how modern farming uses heavy pesticides and chemical fertilizers, often leading farmers themselves to avoid eating their own produce. This realization deepened our resolve to expand chemical-free cultivation.

  • Land expanded from 1 acre to 2.5 acres
  • Protection walls and mesh fencing were built
  • Commitment to chemical-free farming was strengthened

Taming the Name – 2019

In 2019, the farm was formally named BMK’s Sahaja Siddha.

  • Sahaja – Natural, in harmony with nature
  • Siddha – Achieved through experience and practice

COVID-19 Period: Biodiversity Focus

During the COVID-19 period, the focus expanded beyond crops to biodiversity, ecological balance, and soil health. The following were introduced alongside existing cultivation:

  • Nakshatravanam (star-based plantation)
  • Vinayaka Chavithi pooja plants
  • Medicinal plants, native and perennial trees

Tree & Plant Diversity at Sahaja Siddha

Major Trees & Perennials

  • Neem, Jackfruit, Custard Apple (Seethaphal)
  • Ramaphal, Lakshman Phal, Hanuma Phal, Lakshmi Phal
  • Bael (Maredu), Bamboo, Vippa
  • Coconut, Pepper vines, Taiwan Ginger, Noni fruit

Fruit Plants & Specialty Crops

  • Guava – nearly 20 varieties
  • Mango – 10 varieties
  • Peanut Butter Plant, Papaya
  • Banana varieties: Amruthapani, Karupuravalli, Bangla, Yalaki, Rasthali, Nendran, Blue Banana, Batti Green Banana, Old Green Banana
  • Rudraksha, Allspice plant, Biryani leaf

Five-Layer Farming Model – 2020

In 2020, a five-layer farming model was implemented on 1.5 acres, integrating tall trees, medium trees, shrubs, climbers, and ground-cover crops. This improved biodiversity, soil nutrient cycling, and sustainability.

Organic Rice Cultivation Expansion – 2025

  • Initially rice cultivation started on 50 cents
  • Expanded by another 50 cents in the same year
  • Total organic rice cultivation reached 1 acre

Traditional rice varieties such as Kalabatti, Mysore Malliga, Bahurupi, Sivansamba, and other indigenous varieties were grown with zero chemicals.
