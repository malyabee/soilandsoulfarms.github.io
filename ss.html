BMK’s Sahaja Siddha

A Journey from Family Food Needs to Living Biodiversity

The Beginning: Farming Without a Name

BMK’s Sahaja Siddha did not begin as a project or a business. It started quietly, without a name, under the guidance of Beegala Malakondaiah, with one simple objective:

To provide pure and safe food for the family.

In the initial phase, cultivation was limited to nearly 1 acre of land. It included rice, vegetables, fruits, and timber trees. All crops were grown without the use of chemicals. Pesticides, herbicides, and chemical fertilizers were never used. Farming was guided by traditional knowledge and respect for soil as a living entity.

A Turning Point – 2018

In 2018, a conversation with a neighbouring farmer, Penchalaiah, became a defining moment. He explained the extent of chemical usage in modern farming and revealed that many farmers do not consume their own produce due to chemical contamination.

  • Land expanded from 1 acre to 2.5 acres
  • Protective walls and mesh fencing were constructed
  • Commitment to chemical-free farming was strengthened

Naming the Farm – 2019

In 2019, the farm was formally named BMK’s Sahaja Siddha.

  • Sahaja – Natural, in harmony with nature
  • Siddha – Achieved through lived experience

COVID Period: Focus on Biodiversity

During the COVID-19 period, the focus expanded beyond crops to biodiversity, ecological balance, and soil health.

  • Nakshatravanam (star-based tree plantation)
  • Vinayaka Chavithi pooja plants
  • Medicinal plants, native species, and perennial trees

Tree and Plant Diversity

Major Trees & Perennials

  • Neem, Jackfruit, Seethaphal (Custard Apple)
  • Ramaphal, Lakshman Phal, Hanuma Phal, Lakshmi Phal
  • Maredu (Bael), Bamboo, Vippa
  • Coconut, Pepper vines
  • Taiwan Ginger, Noni fruit

Fruit Plants & Specialty Crops

  • Guava – nearly 20 varieties
  • Mango – 10 varieties
  • Peanut Butter Plant, Papaya
  • Banana varieties: Amruthapani, Karupuravalli, Bangla, Yalaki, Rasthali, Nendran, Blue Banana, Batti Green Banana, Old Green Banana
  • Rudraksha, Allspice plant, Biryani leaf

Five-Layer Farming Model – 2020

In 2020, a five-layer farming model was implemented across 1.5 acres, integrating tall trees, medium trees, shrubs, climbers, and ground-cover crops. This improved biodiversity, soil nutrition cycles, and long-term sustainability.

Organic Rice Cultivation Expansion – 2025

  • Initial rice cultivation on 50 cents
  • Expanded by another 50 cents in the same year
  • Total organic rice area reached 1 acre

Traditional rice varieties such as Kalabatti, Mysore Malliga, Bahurupi, Sivansamba, and other indigenous varieties were grown completely without chemicals.

What BMK’s Sahaja Siddha Represents Today

  • Biodiversity-rich natural farming
  • Soil treated as a living system
  • Seed self-reliance
  • Respect and dignity for agriculture
What began as a family food requirement has today become a quiet movement growing in harmony with nature.
