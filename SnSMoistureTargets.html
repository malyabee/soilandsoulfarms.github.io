FSSAI's general spice-powder standard allows moisture up to 12%, but I would not use 12% as your production target. oai_citation:0‡FSSAI

These are recommended internal quality targets, not FSSAI legal limits.

The reason is that moisture % and water activity (aw) are not the same thing. Water activity tells you how much water is actually available for microbial growth. FSSAI's spice guidance specifically discusses water activity for spice powders and recommends dry, well-ventilated storage. oai_citation:1‡FSSAI

2. The biggest mistake to avoid

Don't think:

"We dried the curry leaves properly, therefore the powder is dry."

The final product can pick up moisture during:

drying → cooling → roasting → grinding → mixing → weighing → packing

For example, you may roast curry leaves to 4–5% moisture, but if you leave the powder exposed in Hyderabad's humid environment for 30–60 minutes, it can start absorbing moisture.

So I would design your production around:

DRY → COOL → GRIND → MIX → TEST → PACK

rather than:

DRY → leave open → grind → pack

3. Equipment I strongly recommend

You don't need a laboratory initially.

A. Digital moisture meter

Yes, there are portable food moisture meters available.

For your production unit, I would actually recommend buying a food moisture analyzer / halogen moisture analyzer rather than a cheap grain moisture meter.

There are two categories:

1. Portable moisture meter

  • Quick

  • Easy

  • Good for incoming raw materials

  • Lower cost

  • But accuracy depends heavily on calibration and the product

2. Halogen moisture analyzer

  • Much better for production QC

  • Gives moisture percentage

  • You weigh a sample

  • Instrument heats it

  • Measures weight loss

  • Gives final moisture %

For Soil & Soul Farms, I would choose halogen moisture analyzer as the main QC instrument.

FSSAI's reference moisture method for spices uses drying and measuring weight loss; its laboratory method uses a vacuum oven and analytical balance. oai_citation:2‡FSSAI

4. Even better: Water Activity Meter

If you want to make premium, professional-quality podis, I would eventually add a:

Water Activity (aw) Meter

This is different from a moisture meter.

Think of it this way:

Moisture % = how much water is present

aw = how much of that water is available for microorganisms

That makes aw extremely useful for shelf-life development. The FDA explains that water activity measures the equilibrium moisture condition of the food and is useful for assessing microbial growth potential. oai_citation:3‡U.S. Food and Drug Administration

For your podis, I would aim for a low aw, and then validate the actual safe target through shelf-life testing rather than assuming a universal number.

5. Your production process should look like this

Step 1 — Raw material inspection

For each batch:

  • Sanagala/chana dal

  • Curry leaves

  • Moringa leaves

  • Red chilli

  • Coriander

  • Cumin

  • Sesame/flax seeds, if used

  • Salt

  • Other ingredients

Check:

Appearance → smell → insects → mould → moisture

Do not accept ingredients with mouldy/musty smell.

FSSAI requires spices/herbs to be free from extraneous matter, mould growth and insect infestation. oai_citation:4‡FSSAI

Step 2 — Drying

For curry leaves and moringa:

Wash → drain → dry → low-temperature dehydration → cool

The objective is not simply "make it crispy."

You want:

low moisture + good colour + good aroma + minimal nutrient/volatile-oil loss.

I would strongly prefer a controlled dehydrator over uncontrolled sun drying for your commercial podi production.

Step 3 — Roasting

Roast individual ingredients separately.

For example:

Sanagala Podi

Chana dal
↓
Dry roast
↓
Cool completely
↓
Grind

Do NOT mix hot roasted ingredients together.

6. Cooling is a critical CCP/quality control point

This is something I would specifically put into your Soil & Soul SOP.

After roasting:

Don't immediately grind and pack.

Allow the ingredients to cool to approximately room temperature in a clean, dry, covered food-grade environment.

Why?

Hot product + closed package can produce condensation.

That can create local moisture pockets.

7. Grinding

Grind according to the product's desired texture.

But watch out for:

Heat generation.

High-speed grinding can heat:

  • curry leaves

  • moringa

  • chilli

  • sesame

  • flax

  • oils in roasted ingredients

This can affect:

  • aroma

  • colour

  • flavour

  • oxidation

  • shelf life.

For premium podi, controlled-temperature grinding is preferable.

8. Mixing

After grinding:

Immediately transfer to a closed stainless-steel mixing container.

Don't mix on an open table.

Then take a representative sample.

Test moisture.

For example:

Sanagala Podi Batch SP-001
Moisture = 5.1%

If your internal specification is:

≤6.0% → PASS

If:

6.1–7.0% → HOLD / investigate

If:

>7.0% → REPROCESS / investigate

The exact limits should ultimately be validated with shelf-life testing.

9. Packaging is equally important

This is where many small brands lose shelf life.

For your podis, I would seriously consider:

High-barrier laminated pouch

Something like:

PET / Aluminium foil / PE

or another validated high-barrier food-grade structure.

The objective is to protect against:

  • moisture

  • oxygen

  • light

  • aroma loss

Especially for Karivepaku and Munaga podis, moisture and oxidation protection are important.

11. Don't rely only on moisture

For Soil & Soul Farms, I would establish three levels of testing.

Every production batch

In-house

  • Moisture

  • Appearance

  • Aroma

  • Taste

  • Colour

  • Foreign matter

  • Pack seal

Monthly/periodic

External laboratory

  • Total plate count

  • Yeast & mould

  • Coliform/E. coli as appropriate

  • Salmonella as applicable

  • Aflatoxin where relevant

  • Other contaminants based on ingredients/risk assessment

Product development / shelf-life

Test at:

0 month → 1 → 2 → 3 → 6 → 9 → 12 months

for:

  • Moisture

  • aw

  • microbiology

  • rancidity/oxidation where relevant

  • flavour

  • colour

  • sensory quality

FSSAI's spice guidance recommends regular/periodic testing and says that where an in-house laboratory isn't available, testing should be done through an appropriate accredited laboratory. oai_citation:5‡FSSAI

12. One important point for your three podis

I would not manufacture all three using exactly the same drying process.

🌱 Karivepaku Karam Podi

Main challenge:

curry leaf moisture + colour + aroma

Need very good leaf dehydration.

🌿 Munaga Karam Podi

Main challenge:

moringa leaf drying + green colour + bitterness + moisture pickup

Controlled drying will make a big difference.

🫘 Sanagala Podi

Main challenge:

roasted chana + chilli/spices + oxidation

Here roasting and cooling become particularly important.
